# Copywriting

Copy has one reader and one job. The reader is the customer the product is for, seeing it in one placement while half paying attention. The job is one action. A first pass is never the deliverable.

## Before writing

Write these down for yourself before drafting:

- **Buyer.** Who pays or acts, concretely: what they do, what they are trying to get done, and the words they use for it.
- **Placement.** Where it runs, what surrounds it, how long they look, and the character limits.
- **Pain.** What they already feel, in their words, and what they tried that failed.
- **Proof.** The specific thing this product does that their current option doesn't. A number, a capability, a name.
- **Action.** One.
- **Venue rules.** Read the current ad or platform policy: banned categories, trademark use, landing-page review. Denied copy runs zero times.

## What great looks like

- The buyer reads it as a peer talking in their vocabulary and thinks "this is for me".
- One concrete promise they can picture. Specific beats clever.
- Only this product could say it. If it still works with a competitor's name swapped in, rewrite it.
- Plain and confident. No puns, wordplay, cute imperatives ("Just ask."), invented rhetorical questions, or social-post voice.
- Every claim is true today. Never invent numbers, results, reviews, coverage or prices.

## Writers room

1. Draft at least five candidates, each from a different angle: the pain, the outcome, the proof, the buyer's identity, and the contrast with what they use now.
2. Cold-read each one as the buyer, in the placement, in two seconds. Score it 1 to 10 on fit for this buyer, concrete promise, only-us, clarity at a glance, truth and craft. Most drafts are a 5. Quote every weak word and write its fix. Each writing tic costs a point.
3. Rewrite the best few from the notes, take the best lines from the rest, and score again. Run at least three rounds and stop when the best scores 8 or higher.
4. Deliver only the finals, each with one line on why it works for the buyer.

## Never

- Category descriptions in place of a promise ("Live sports data for your chats").
- Filler: elevate, unlock, seamless, supercharge, game-changer, level up, effortless, powerful, journey.
- Throat clearing, hedges, and a slogan bolted on at the end.
- Writing tics: load the writing-tics skill and check every line.
